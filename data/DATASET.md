# Dataset: the simulated claims world

This project uses no external dataset. Every experiment runs on a synthetic insurance-claims world generated by `src/agentaudit/world.py` from one integer seed. The same seed always rebuilds the same world, which is what makes replay and ground truth possible.

## Why synthetic

The research questions need facts that no public dataset records:

- which inputs actually determined each decision;
- which policy violations were injected, and where;
- how every data source changed after each decision.

A real claims log would give us decisions. It would not tell us whether a replay six months later followed the original path. The price of this choice is external validity: the results describe this world and this agent. Section 5 of `research/REPORT.md` discusses what transfers.

## Scale per seed

| Item | Default | Where set |
|---|---|---|
| Claims | 2,000, arriving uniformly over days 0–180 | `World(n_claims=…)`, `CLAIM_DAYS` |
| Policies and holders | 900 | `World(n_policies=…)` |
| Horizon (environment keeps changing until) | day 360 | `HORIZON_DAYS` |
| Historical claims used to train the first fraud model | 3,000, days −360 to 0 | `_train_models` |
| Calendar | day 0 = 2026-01-01 (UTC) | `BASE_DATE` |

E1 and E2 use 10 seeds (20,000 decisions). E3 uses 20 seeds, each run with both the faulty and the clean agent (40,000 + 40,000 decisions). E4 uses 5 seeds of 300 claims. E5 uses 3 seeds of 2,000. Base seed 20261008 throughout (configs in `configs/`).

## Entities and generating process

**Policies.**
- Three products, each with sections:
  - travel: baggage, medical, cancellation;
  - home: theft, water, fire;
  - gadget: damage, device theft.
- Each section has a limit drawn uniformly from a product-specific range, rounded to EUR 100, and at most one exclusion (unattended, pre-existing, known event, wear and tear).
- Deductible is 50, 100 or 250 EUR.
- Policies start between day −200 and day 29 and normally run 365 days. 10% are cancelled early, with an end day in [0, 150).
- 30% of policies receive one revision at a uniform day in [0, 360]: a limit change (×0.6–1.3), a new deductible (100/250/500), or an added "unattended" exclusion.

**Claims.**
- A claim is against a random policy and one of its sections.
- **Fraud:** 8% of claims are fraudulent.
  - A fraudulent claim inflates the true loss by ×1.3–2.4.
  - It is reported later: the reporting delay is geometric with mean 14 days for fraud and 7 days otherwise.
  - It lacks receipts more often: 30% of fraudulent claims versus 8% of genuine ones.
- **True loss:** log-normal around a section median (baggage 700, medical 2,500, … fire 6,000 EUR), with σ = 0.75.
- **Currency:** 62% EUR, 12% USD, 10% GBP, 6% CHF, 5% JPY, 5% INR.
- **Receipts:** split into 1–3 parts.
  - 35% of genuine claimants have receipts covering only 50–100% of the loss.
  - 40% of fraudsters forge receipts matching the inflated amount.
  - So receipts alone do not reveal fraud.
- **Theft claims:** 85% include a police report.
- **Free-text description:** built from template sentences. Fraudulent claims, and 5% of genuine claims, include inconsistent sentences.
- **Amendments:** 10% of claims are amended 5–120 days after submission. The amendment adds a receipt and raises the amount by up to 30%.
- **Outcomes:** each claim's outcome (fraud confirmed or not) becomes known 20–70 days after submission.

**Exchange rates.** Each foreign currency follows a daily geometric random walk with 0.6% daily volatility from a fixed start rate.

**Fraud model.**
- A gradient-boosted classifier (scikit-learn `HistGradientBoostingClassifier`) on 15 claim features.
- Retrained every 30 days on the historical claims plus every claim whose outcome was known by that day.
- All versions are kept in a registry.
- A second model trained with holder age exists only so the faulty agent can misuse it (violation V7).

**Agent versions.**
- `claims-agent@1.0` on day 0: fraud threshold 0.5, approval limit 5,000 EUR.
- `@1.1` on day 75: 0.45 and 4,000 EUR.
- `@1.2` on day 150: narrative threshold 0.65 instead of 0.7, minimum narrative length 140 instead of 160, QA sampling 4% instead of 5%.

**External narrative assessor.** It scores a description for inconsistency. Its noise (σ = 0.08) comes from its own random stream, so it returns a different score on every call and cannot be re-run.

**Adjusters.**
- Eight adjusters, each picked at random from those who did not handle intake.
- Each decides correctly with probability 0.92.
- A human decision cannot be re-requested during an audit.

## What changes after a decision (drift sources)

| Source | What a live lookup on a later day sees |
|---|---|
| `fx.spot` | that day's rate |
| `policy_store.get` | the revised policy, if a revision happened since |
| `claims_db.get` | the amended claim, if amended since |
| `holders.profile` | a larger prior-claim count and a longer tenure |
| fraud model | the version current on that day |
| agent configuration | the version current on that day |
| `narrative.assess` | a new random score |
| adjuster | cannot be asked again |

## Decision types

Each decision is labelled with the hardest dependency on its path. This is simulation ground truth, not something the agent records. The precedence is:

1. human with random QA sampling (**stochastic**);
2. human or automatic with the narrative score within 0.1 of its threshold, or a failed narrative rule (**opaque**);
3. fraud score within 0.1 of its threshold, or failed (**model**);
4. other escalations (**human**);
5. automatic denial by a policy rule (**rule**);
6. automatic payment in a foreign currency (**fx**);
7. automatic payment in EUR (**calculation**).

Counts per run are in the E1 tables.

## Injected violations (E3)

On each claim, `FaultyClaimsAgent` attempts one violation with probability 0.4. The type is chosen uniformly from 13 kinds. The attempt only takes effect when the claim makes it possible: for example, `limit_exceeded` needs a verified amount above the limit. So the realised violation rate is much lower than 0.4 (see the "Injected" column in E3).

The plan for each claim is a deterministic function of (seed, claim id). The faulty agent's decisions can therefore be replayed with the faulty code itself.

| Violation | What the agent does |
|---|---|
| approval_bypass | pays above the approval limit without escalation |
| unsupported_denial | denies a payable claim citing an exclusion that does not apply |
| limit_exceeded | ignores the section limit in the payout |
| calc_input_mismatch | omits the deductible from the payout calculation |
| unverified_amount | pays the claimed amount when the receipts total less |
| fraud_escalation_skipped | does not escalate a fraud score over threshold |
| prohibited_feature | scores fraud with a model that uses holder age |
| stale_rate | converts with a rate 5–20 days old |
| unauthorised_tool | calls `social.profile`, which is not on the allowlist |
| segregation_of_duties | the intake handler approves the escalated claim |
| missing_checks | skips the exclusion and reporting-window rules |
| rate_operand_mismatch (held out) | converts the verified amount with a rate 3% off the quoted one |
| evidence_substitution (held out) | counts a receipt from another claim |

The two held-out violations were written after the audit standard's checks had been fixed. They measure what generic checks catch without a check written for the fault.

## Limitations

- The decision logic is a small rule-plus-model pipeline, not a language-model agent. Its steps are explicit, which makes the L3 and L4 traces complete by construction. An LLM agent's internal reasoning would not be.
- Drift rates (revision, amendment, FX volatility, retraining interval) are chosen, not estimated. E2 shows how results move with audit delay, which is the main lever these rates act through.
- Adjuster behaviour is a coin flip with a fixed accuracy. It matters here only because a human decision cannot be re-derived.
